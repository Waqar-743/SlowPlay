# Kharpocho reconstruction: evidence and interpretation

Research checked on 29 October 2026. This document distinguishes the references used to understand the place from the geometry created to communicate it.

## Purpose and scope

The project interprets Kharpocho as a fort inseparable from its rocky setting: an irregular, largely barren hill between Skardu and the Indus, carrying an enclosure on an eastern shoulder beneath a higher ridge. The complete landform is included so its defensive position can be understood from base to summit and from different directions.

The model is not a scientific terrain product, archaeological plan or reconstruction of one exact historical year. Published descriptions and photographs establish broad relationships and characteristic architecture. Modelling fills the space between those references. Its internal coordinates, slopes, room sizes, stones and paths are approximate.

No survey DEM, satellite elevation raster, georeferenced orthophoto, laser scan, measured floor plan or photogrammetric mesh was incorporated. A reference image is not an elevation survey: foreshortening, lens perspective and incomplete visibility prevent exact inference of dimensions and hidden faces. The nominal metre scale supports consistent scene proportions only.

## Sources

### 1. Department of Archaeology and Museums, Pakistan — Kharpocho Fort Complex

[DOAM site 7818](https://doam.gov.pk/public/sites/7818)

Used for the eastern spur, higher watchtower remains, southern access, northern river relationship and water-storage provision. This record also describes rebuilding and distinguishes the surviving fabric from an older fort. It is a heritage inventory description, not a measured plan.

### 2. Department of Archaeology and Museums, Pakistan — Kharphocho Fort

[DOAM site 8178](https://doam.gov.pk/public/sites/8178)

Used for the two functional levels, courtyard pond, cator-and-cribbage construction, adobe battlements, firing niches and Panjtan mosque. The stated wall thickness informs a visually substantial enclosure rather than a uniformly thin wall. The source does not locate each documented function on a complete surveyed footprint.

### 3. Max Klimburg — Traditional Art and Architecture in Baltistan

[Chapter PDF in the Archnet archive](https://s3.amazonaws.com/media.archnet.org/system/publications/contents/4874/original/DPC1587.pdf?1384787082=)

The chapter explains timber reinforcement, interlocking beams and stone or earth infill in Baltistani building. Printed pages 151–152 discuss cator and cribbage; pages 155–156 include the Skardu fort mosque and fig. 139, based on a 1914 photograph. It provides regional architectural context and evidence for a substantial corbelled mosque cornice. Related monuments are comparisons, not templates copied into Kharpocho. A generic palace, European turret system or intact decorative roof has not been inferred simply from regional parallels.

### 4. Hannan Balti — Aerial view of Kharpocho Fort

[Original file and author record on Wikimedia Commons](https://commons.wikimedia.org/wiki/File:Aerial_view_of_Kharpocho_Fort.jpg)

Photographed 1 April 2018. Used to study enclosure, ridge and landscape relationships. Although titled “aerial,” the author describes photographing from the mountain's top; it should not be treated as an orthographic drone survey. The original source photograph is supplied unchanged as `public/references/aerial.jpg`. Licence: [CC BY-SA 4.0](https://creativecommons.org/licenses/by-sa/4.0/). ## Conflicts and unresolved questions

The two official inventory records do not present one consistent chronology. Site 7818 assigns a sixteenth-century relative chronology and discusses a later rebuilding; site 8178 assigns a fourteenth-to-fifteenth-century chronology and names different construction and intervention stages. They also give inconsistent or unclear height descriptions. This project does not choose one entry as a definitive founding date or convert either description into a precise terrain elevation.

The resulting scene combines surviving character and restrained completion of missing fabric. It should be described as a contemporary researched interpretation with historic references, not as “Kharpocho in 1490,” “the original fort,” or a fully authenticated pre-destruction state. Rebuilding, loss and repair have changed the monument over time, and photographs from different dates do not necessarily show the same condition.

The following remain uncertain in the model:

- Exact mountain perimeter, height, cross-sections and hidden cliff geometry.
- Absolute fort dimensions, orientation of individual rooms and detailed wall thickness changes.
- Precise positions and extents of residential rooms, barracks, storage, reservoirs and vanished passages.
- Roof completeness, mosque crown and proportions of missing timber work.
- The original form and successive changes of the gate and approach.
- Age, cause and distribution of the damage shown in generated masonry.
- River position, braiding and water colour for any particular season or historical date.
- Historical vegetation, settlement density and building distribution in the valley.

## Material and landscape interpretation

Rock and stone variants use the 2K photogrammetry PBR set [Rock Face, Poly Haven](https://polyhaven.com/a/rock_face), processed by Dario Barresi and photographed by Greg Zaal, under [CC0](https://polyhaven.com/license). The set provides colour, OpenGL normal, roughness, ambient occlusion and height maps. Colour was desaturated and brightened for the reconstruction's rock/stone variants. This is a general rock reference material, not a scan of the actual Kharpocho hill or its masonry.

Other maps—sand, plaster and timber—are original procedural studies. Their surface variation conveys age and scale without claiming a geological or conservation diagnosis. Larger folds, ridges and fractures are modelled geometry. Material provenance metadata should retain the Poly Haven asset title and URL, processing and photography credits, CC0 licence, 2K map specification and recorded colour adjustments separately from the original geometry's CC BY 4.0 attribution.

The palette stays within weathered greys, charcoal, sand and dusty brown. Vegetation is concentrated sparsely on the valley floor; exposed mountain slopes remain predominantly barren. Golden-hour light, haze and a small amount of drifting dust serve presentation. They are not evidence of a historic climate, a specific time of year or measured atmospheric conditions.

## Updating the reconstruction responsibly

A future measured survey should replace the terrain and building controls before finer decorative additions are treated as improvements in authenticity. Useful next inputs would include a licensed georeferenced terrain model, photographs covering all hill faces, a conservation plan with dimensions, documented mosque and gate surveys, and dated images that distinguish successive building phases.

When a feature changes, record its source and whether it is observed, described, inferred or contextual. Keep the reference files and licences separate from newly created assets. Preserve this uncertainty statement when sharing renders, models or educational derivatives.
