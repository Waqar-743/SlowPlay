KHARPOCHO / FIELD NOTES

THE LANDSCAPE & THE EVIDENCE

Reading the
mountain.

Kharpocho belongs to its rock. Its walls follow the summit’s irregular edge, overlooking the Indus and the settlement of Skardu.

A fort in two levels

Pakistan’s Department of Archaeology and Museums describes upper residential and lower military areas, a water reservoir and the Panjtan mosque. These references guide the arrangement of the summit complex.

Stone, earth and timber

Traditional cator-and-cribbage construction binds heavy masonry with timber. The model uses visible timber bands, irregular stone courses and weathered earth finishes to interpret this Balti building tradition.

KHARPOCHO / THE RECONSTRUCTION

A PLACE, CAREFULLY INTERPRETED

Between evidence
and imagination.

A navigable study of Kharpocho Fort and its complete mountain, made to invite a closer look at Skardu’s remarkable heritage.

01

Start with the landscape

The entire isolated rocky mass is modelled in three dimensions, from the foot of its slopes to the summit. Ridges, fractured cliffs, scree and the climbing path give the stronghold its setting.

02

Follow the surviving evidence

Published descriptions and photographs inform the stone-and-timber construction, fortified entrance, irregular enclosing walls and mosque. The structure sits within the summit rock rather than on an invented platform.

03

Make uncertainty visible

This is a historically informed interpretation, not a measured reconstruction or a claim about the fort at one exact date. Building dimensions, lost interiors, detailed terrain and some ruin arrangements are inferred.

04

Keep the landscape explorable

The scene uses reusable geometry, material textures and instanced masonry. Four camera studies, free orbit and a guided journey reveal different relationships between architecture, mountain and river.
